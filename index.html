Data Model

9 tables, one star: Campaigns, Customers and Dim_Date in the middle

Campaigns → Emails → Activities is the email funnel (Campaign_ID and Email_ID, exactly as in the KPI document). The same Campaign_ID also feeds Social_Ads_Daily (Facebook, Instagram) and WhatsApp_Messages. Customers and Orders add who and how much (Orders point back to the email or WhatsApp message that got the last click), and Web_Engagement is aggregated site traffic that joins only through Dim_Date. Knowing what you can't join is as important as the joins.

Watch out for this

Common QA mistakes on this project's KPIs

Before you sign off on any KPI card or chart, check for these

  • Open rate uses distinct openers, not Open rows; show the human (non-machine) open rate too.
  • Delivery Rate = Delivered ÷ Recipients, a %, not delivered activities ÷ emails (369.5).
  • Web rates are session-weighted: SUM(Bounced_Sessions) ÷ SUM(Sessions), never AVERAGE(Bounce_Rate_Pct).
  • Shares use SUM(Sessions) and ALL(), never a row count (12.5% for every source).
  • Unique_Visitors is not additive: label the sum visitor-days.
  • Revenue = Delivered orders; spend = Actual_Spend_INR.
  • Reconcile every KPI against the equivalent SQL query before presenting.

Interview Trap

Key difference: Open Rate vs. CTR vs. CTOR

Three ratios built from the same events that answer different questions. Mixing them up (or counting rows instead of people) is the most common email-metrics mistake in interviews.

MetricFormula (this project)What it measuresAXon value
Open Rate (unique)Distinct openers ÷ deliveredSubject line + sender + inbox placement. Human version removes Apple Mail machine opens.45.42% (human 31.58%)
CTR (unique)Distinct clickers ÷ deliveredThe whole email's pull: did it make people visit?6.55%
CTORDistinct clickers ÷ distinct openersContent quality among people who opened.14.43%
KPI-doc "CTR"Click rows ÷ Open rowsA click-to-open ratio of activity counts (repeat rows included).12.76%
